# Phase 2 — Consolidation and Knowledge Assembly

## Purpose

Use the learner's generated RSMs as raw material for explicit instruction. Compare them fairly, explain their limits causally, and assemble the canonical account so every formal component has a visible reason to exist.

## Announce the transition

```text
PHASE: CONSOLIDATION AND KNOWLEDGE ASSEMBLY
LEARNING TARGET: <target>
YOUR CURRENT TASK: Examine how your attempts reveal the target structure.
```

## 1. Reconstruct the learner's RSMs

Summarize each important RSM in language the learner can recognize. Preserve its strongest reasonable interpretation. Distinguish what the learner said from tutor inference, and request correction when reconstruction is uncertain.

Do not caricature an incorrect approach to make the canonical account look inevitable.

## 2. Compare and contrast

For each important RSM, identify:

- prior knowledge activated;
- what it captured successfully;
- affordances and constraints;
- cases in which it works;
- cases in which it fails;
- critical features noticed and omitted;
- useful components it can contribute to the canonical account.

Use a compact table when several repeated fields make relationships easier to inspect. Keep causal explanation in prose around it.

`SHOW THE CONTRAST` stops here: compare the RSMs and ask what the learner notices, without completing the canonical account.

## 3. Explain why gaps matter

Do not merely label a method wrong. Explain which requirement it violates, why the limitation occurs, why an omitted feature is necessary, and under what restricted conditions the RSM remains valid.

Differentiate:

- incorrect assumption;
- reasonable approximation;
- incomplete model;
- implementation or calculation error;
- unsuitable representation;
- missing prerequisite.

## 4. Assemble the canonical account

Use this sequence:

```text
learner language and attempts
→ critical features and contrasts
→ formal organization
→ canonical account
```

For every canonical component, point to the requirement, tension, failed case, or useful learner idea that makes it necessary. Introduce formal terms, notation, representations, procedures, and conventions now—not retroactively in the learner's earlier RSMs.

## 5. Teach explicitly

Once the need is visible, provide:

- a precise definition or governing principle;
- the canonical representation or procedure;
- a worked example with the reason behind each important step;
- boundary conditions and typical misconceptions;
- connections to the learner's prior knowledge and RSMs.

Explicit instruction is required here. Showing a polished answer without explaining its organization is insufficient.

## 6. Require learner reconstruction

Ask the learner, in their own words, to explain:

- what problem the concept solves;
- why its critical features are necessary;
- how the canonical account is assembled;
- how it differs from their earlier RSMs;
- when it applies and when it does not.

Correct misunderstandings explicitly. If the learner can repeat terminology but cannot explain the contrasts, revisit the relevant RSM-to-feature connection.

## Completion gate

Advance to `transfer` only when:

- important RSMs have been reconstructed fairly;
- their affordances and constraints have been explained causally;
- the canonical account has been assembled from exposed needs;
- explicit instruction and a worked example are complete;
- the learner has reconstructed the target reasoning with no unresolved foundational misconception;
- the ledger records evidence and uncertainty separately.

Then announce the transfer phase and load only its reference.
