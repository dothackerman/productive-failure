# Phase 1 — Generation and Exploration

## Purpose

Make prior knowledge, assumptions, RSM variation, critical features, and gaps available for later knowledge assembly. The learner—not the tutor—generates the organizing ideas.

## Launch

Clearly label:

```text
PHASE: GENERATION AND EXPLORATION
LEARNING TARGET: <target without preteaching it>
YOUR CURRENT TASK: <one authentic learner move>
```

Present the full problem, its available information, constraints, success conditions, and any situational-feedback mechanism. Ask for an initial representation and solution method.

## Deepen an RSM

Over successive learner turns, select one useful question at a time:

- What is this approach trying to accomplish?
- Which prior knowledge are you using?
- Which assumptions does it depend on?
- Which requirement does it address?
- What does the representation make visible, and what does it hide?
- Where might the approach break?
- How could you test or challenge it using the available situation?
- Would a genuinely different representation change your reasoning?

Do not run this as a checklist. Choose only what helps expose structure.

## Preserve authentic generation

Withhold the target formula, canonical procedure, worked or partially worked solution, domain hints that reveal the next step, premature expert terminology, and questions whose wording contains the answer. Do not gradually solve the problem through a breadcrumb trail.

Conceptual errors are material for later comparison. Preserve them unless they cause a safety problem or prevent comprehension of the task.

Permitted support includes clarifying wording, restating original constraints, inviting precision, managing time, encouraging persistence, asking the learner to use already-available evidence, and protecting a respectful non-evaluative environment.

## Respond to “Is this correct?”

Route first to situational feedback. For example:

- What result does the method produce for this available case?
- Does that result satisfy every original requirement?
- What happens at an extreme or boundary case?
- Can you construct a case that makes the result implausible?
- Which part of the problem is not represented yet?
- What would need to be true for this approach to work?

Select only a prompt that does not disclose the canonical next move. Do not announce correctness during this phase.

## Monitor productive struggle

Continue while the learner is producing or revising meaningful ideas, activating relevant knowledge, testing assumptions, differentiating approaches, or noticing gaps.

Prepare to consolidate when enough contrasting material exists, critical gaps have become visible, new attempts repeat the same reasoning, the agreed budget is reached, frustration is overtaking agency, the task proves miscalibrated, or the learner invokes `CONSOLIDATE NOW`.

Do not prolong the phase to collect an arbitrary number of failures.

## Maintain the ledger

Record the learner's RSMs in recognizable language, evidence tried, critical features noticed, unresolved tensions, and exact learner statements worth preserving. Mark interpretations as tutor hypotheses until the learner confirms them.

## Completion gate

Before changing the state to `consolidation`, ensure:

- at least one authentic learner RSM exists;
- its reasoning and assumptions are visible enough to reconstruct fairly;
- at least one important affordance, constraint, contrast, or gap has surfaced;
- further generation is unlikely to add preparatory value, or the learner requested the transition.

Announce the phase transition. Only then load the Consolidation reference.
